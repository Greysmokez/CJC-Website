The Jubilee Calendar and the Story of the End

A Bible Prophecy Timeline: God’s Clock, the Seals, the Trumpets and the Bowls

“Blessed is he that readeth, and they that hear the words of this prophecy, and keep those things which are written therein: for the time is at hand.” — Revelation 1:3 (KJV)

How to Use This Paper

This paper is the doorway to the rest of my research on the end of the story. It walks through the whole picture once, in order, so that a first-time reader can see how the pieces fit before going deeper. Each section ends by pointing to the paper that covers it in full.

If you remember only one thing, remember this pattern. As I read it, Revelation tells the end of the story in three layers:

  • The seals are Christ’s missions. He opens them one at a time, and each one sends out His work into the world.
  • The trumpets are God’s warnings. They sound inside the seals, and each one strikes only a part of the earth, because God is still calling people to turn back to Him.
  • The bowls are God’s wrath. They are poured out only after the warnings are over and the door of mercy has closed, and they fall on those who took the mark of the beast.

And through all three, God keeps His own. He has done it every time before, from Noah to Egypt to Babylon, and He will do it again.

A few words before we begin. A covenant is a solemn agreement between God and His people. A Jubilee is the fiftieth year in Israel’s calendar, when slaves went free and land went back to its owners. The great tribulation is the final time of trouble before Christ returns. Babylon in Revelation is not the ancient city but a world power that sets itself against God. Other terms are explained as they come up.

1. The Big Idea: A Promise Kept

The Bible’s story begins and ends with one promise. God said to Israel: “And I will walk among you, and will be your God, and ye shall be my people” (Leviticus 26:12). Israel agreed to it at Mount Sinai: “All that the LORD hath spoken we will do” (Exodus 19:8). And the last pages of the Bible show the promise kept: “Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God” (Revelation 21:3).

There is a problem in the middle of the story. People keep breaking their side of the promise. They needed someone to rescue them and make the promise last, and God sent Him: “and thou shalt call his name JESUS: for he shall save his people from their sins” (Matthew 1:21). His two comings, the first to save and the second to finish the rescue, are the backbone of this whole paper.

What happens when God’s people ignore Him? Scripture shows the same lesson many times: outward religion without an inward heart leads to captivity. Jesus quoted Isaiah about it: “This people draweth nigh unto me with their mouth, and honoureth me with their lips; but their heart is far from me” (Matthew 15:8). When Israel and Judah kept refusing to listen, they were carried away as captives (2 Kings 17:7-23; 2 Chronicles 36:15-19).

One command they neglected was rest. God told them to let the land rest every seventh year (Leviticus 25:1-7), and He warned them what would happen if they did not: “Then shall the land enjoy her sabbaths, as long as it lieth desolate, and ye be in your enemies’ land; even then shall the land rest, and enjoy her sabbaths. As long as it lieth desolate it shall rest; because it did not rest in your sabbaths, when ye dwelt upon it” (Leviticus 26:34-35).

That is exactly what happened. Judah was taken to Babylon, and the land lay empty “until the land had enjoyed her sabbaths: for as long as she lay desolate she kept sabbath, to fulfil threescore and ten years” (2 Chronicles 36:21). Seventy rest years were paid back. Since a rest year comes once in every seven, seventy missed rest years stand for 490 years of neglect (70 × 7 = 490). Scripture gives the seventy; the 490 is simple arithmetic. Keep that number in mind, because it comes back in Daniel’s seventy weeks.

God’s rest laws come in three sizes. Every seventh day is a Sabbath (Exodus 20:8-11). Every seventh year is a sabbath year for the land (Leviticus 25:1-7). And after seven of those, the fiftieth year is the Jubilee, when “ye shall hallow the fiftieth year, and proclaim liberty throughout all the land unto all the inhabitants thereof” (Leviticus 25:10). The pattern is always the same: work, then rest, then a fresh start.

This paper asks a simple question. If we count Israel’s years by that same pattern, without a break, does the count fit the big story of the Bible, from the Messiah’s first coming to His return? It does not try to name the day Jesus will return, because He said no one knows it (Matthew 24:36).

2. God’s Clock: The Continuous Jubilee Calendar

God gave Israel a calendar of years: “And thou shalt number seven sabbaths of years unto thee, seven times seven years; and the space of the seven sabbaths of years shall be unto thee forty and nine years” (Leviticus 25:8).

The Continuous Jubilee Calendar, or CJC, is simply that count kept without a break. It works like this:

  • The years are counted in blocks, or cycles, of 49.
  • The first year of every cycle is a Jubilee. The fiftieth year of one cycle is also the first year of the next.
  • Inside each cycle, the years come in weeks of seven. The seventh year of each week is a sabbath year, when the land rests.
  • The middle year of each week of years is called Position 4.
  • A CJC year runs from spring to spring, as God told Israel: “This month shall be unto you the beginning of months: it shall be the first month of the year to you” (Exodus 12:2).
  • The Jubilee itself is announced with the trumpet on the Day of Atonement, in the seventh month (Leviticus 25:9). So a Jubilee year is numbered from spring, while its rest from sowing runs from that autumn to the next autumn.

Anyone can check every date with one formula. For any year, B = the year + 1437 (for AD years), or 1438 − the year (for BC years). Then Cycle = (B − 1) ÷ 49, rounded down, + 1, and the Year in the cycle = B − (Cycle − 1) × 49. Year 1 is a Jubilee. For the full method and how it was tested, see Chapter 1: The Jubilee Calendar Explained.

The first check: Hezekiah’s sign. When Assyria’s army surrounded Judah, Isaiah gave King Hezekiah a sign: “And this shall be a sign unto thee, Ye shall eat this year such as groweth of itself; and the second year that which springeth of the same: and in the third year sow ye, and reap, and plant vineyards, and eat the fruit thereof” (Isaiah 37:30). Two years in a row without sowing is exactly what the calendar shows: 703 BC was a sabbath year (Cycle 15, Year 49), and 702 BC was a Jubilee. The Assyrian attack and God’s rescue came in 701 BC, while the land was still in its Jubilee rest, which had begun in the autumn of 702 BC (Leviticus 25:9; Isaiah 37:36). I treat this as a strong fit, not a proof, because some scholars think the war itself left the fields empty.

The second check: the Messiah. Five cycles later, 457 BC is also a Jubilee. That is the year of Artaxerxes’ decree to restore Jerusalem, which starts Daniel’s count. Counted from there, the count reaches the Messiah’s anointing in AD 27, the cross in the middle year of the last week in AD 30, and the end of the seventy weeks with the next Jubilee in AD 34. Section 4 walks through Gabriel’s message to Daniel step by step.

What counts as a test. The calendar is anchored to 457 BC and AD 30, so their places on it are not independent proof. The real tests are dates the anchors did not set. Hezekiah’s two years without sowing, 703 and 702 BC, land on a sabbath year followed by a Jubilee, five cycles before 457 BC. And the end of the 1,260 years in 1798 falls at the turn of a Jubilee, exactly 36 cycles after AD 34. I offer these as strong fits for anyone to test, not as proofs that settle the matter.

One unbroken count. From the Jubilee of 702 BC to the Jubilee of 1994 is exactly 55 cycles, with no gap and no adjustment. The count runs straight through Hezekiah’s day, the exile, Daniel’s seventy weeks, the cross, AD 70 and the long centuries after. When Jesus began His ministry, He read from Isaiah about “the acceptable year of the Lord” (Luke 4:19; Isaiah 61:2), the language of the Jubilee. The calendar He grew up under is the one Scripture’s timeline runs on.

The count begins in 1437 BC, which the math reaches when it is worked backward from 457 BC and AD 30. That year is Year 1 of the first cycle, a counting start only; it is not itself counted as a Jubilee, and it is not a claim about the date of the Exodus. Seventy full cycles of 49 years were complete in the spring of 1994, which opened the seventieth Jubilee (Cycle 71, Year 1).

3. Two Ways God Measures Prophetic Time

Before we look at the end, we need to know how Scripture counts its prophetic numbers. It counts them in two ways, and Scripture gives us the clues to tell them apart.

A day for a year. In long prophecies told in pictures, covering many generations, God counts a day for a year. He set the rule Himself: “After the number of the days in which ye searched the land, even forty days, each day for a year, shall ye bear your iniquities, even forty years” (Numbers 14:34), and “I have appointed thee each day for a year” (Ezekiel 4:6). That is how Daniel’s seventy weeks became 490 years. It is also how two prophecies of persecution became 1,260 years. Daniel saw a power that would “wear out the saints of the most High, and think to change times and laws: and they shall be given into his hand until a time and times and the dividing of time” (Daniel 7:25). John saw the same years from the church’s side: “And the woman fled into the wilderness, where she hath a place prepared of God, that they should feed her there a thousand two hundred and threescore days” (Revelation 12:6). Those 1,260 years are commonly dated from AD 538 to 1798.

Literal days. The prophecies still ahead are different. They describe the same people living through them from beginning to end, in words that only make sense as days. The days are shortened so that people survive them: “And except that the Lord had shortened those days, no flesh should be saved: but for the elect’s sake, whom he hath chosen, he hath shortened the days” (Mark 13:20). The same men are tormented for “five months” (Revelation 9:5). Crowds watch the two witnesses lie dead “three days and an half” (Revelation 11:9). And a blessing waits for the one who lives to reach the last day of the count: “Blessed is he that waiteth, and cometh to the thousand three hundred and five and thirty days” (Daniel 12:12). If these were years, the people Revelation describes would grow old and die long before the periods ended.

When does the change come? This is this research’s framework, and it rests on two supports. The first is the wording of the prophecies themselves: as shown above, the end-time periods describe the same people living through them from start to finish, which only makes sense as days. The second explains when the change comes. As I read it, the day-for-a-year rule belongs to the long era while God’s Jubilee count was running, and that count was complete when the seventieth 49-year cycle ended in the spring of 1994. Scripture uses seventy again and again for a measure brought to its end: the seventy years of exile “to fulfil threescore and ten years” (2 Chronicles 36:21), the “seventy weeks” determined “to finish the transgression” (Daniel 9:24), and seventy who went down into Egypt (Genesis 46:27). The number seventy does not prove the change by itself. It marks when the long count is complete, and the wording of the prophecies shows that what follows is counted in days. The year 1994 does not date anything still ahead. It marks the close of one way of counting, not the start of the end.

4. What Has Already Been Fulfilled

Before looking ahead, it helps to see how faithfully God has already kept His word, because the same God gave the prophecies still waiting.

Gabriel’s sevens. Gabriel’s message to Daniel is built entirely out of sevens. The word translated “weeks” means “sevens” (Hebrew note, outside the KJV text), and in this prophecy each seven is a week of years, the same seven-year unit as Israel’s sabbath-year cycle: “Six years thou shalt sow thy field... But in the seventh year shall be a sabbath of rest unto the land” (Leviticus 25:3-4).

Daniel was ready to hear it in exactly those terms. He had just been reading Jeremiah: “I Daniel understood by books the number of the years, whereof the word of the LORD came to Jeremiah the prophet, that he would accomplish seventy years in the desolations of Jerusalem” (Daniel 9:2). He knew why the exile lasted seventy years: the land had to keep the sabbath years it had been robbed of (2 Chronicles 36:21; Leviticus 26:34-35). And he confessed the very curse of Leviticus 26 by name: “therefore the curse is poured upon us, and the oath that is written in the law of Moses the servant of God, because we have sinned against him” (Daniel 9:11). That is the chapter where God warned four times, “I will punish you seven times more for your sins” (Leviticus 26:18; also 26:21, 24, 28).

So Gabriel answered him in the language of the sabbath years:

Why “seven weeks” matters. Gabriel did not just say sixty-nine weeks. He split off the first seven, and forty-nine years is exactly “the space of the seven sabbaths of years” (Leviticus 25:8), one full Jubilee cycle. A man who had just confessed breaking the sabbath-year law, and who was reading Jeremiah’s count of the land’s missed rests, would have heard it at once: this countdown is measured on God’s Jubilee calendar. As I read it, that told Daniel the count would start where a Jubilee cycle starts, with the decree going out in a Jubilee year. Scripture does not say in so many words that Daniel worked out the year. It gives him the measure, and the calendar shows the decree of 457 BC landing on it: Cycle 21, Year 1, a Jubilee.

“In the midst of the week.” The last seven runs from AD 27, when Jesus was anointed, through AD 33, a sabbath year (Cycle 30, Years 43 to 49). Its middle year, Position 4, is AD 30, with three years of the week before it and three after it. That is where the cross falls, just as Gabriel said: “And he shall confirm the covenant with many for one week: and in the midst of the week he shall cause the sacrifice and the oblation to cease” (Daniel 9:27). Jesus used Daniel’s own words at the Last Supper: “For this is my blood of the new testament, which is shed for many for the remission of sins” (Matthew 26:28). When He died, “the veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom” (Matthew 27:51). The priests went on offering for forty more years, but the offerings had lost their purpose: “Now where remission of these is, there is no more offering for sin” (Hebrews 10:18).

The week completed. The seventy weeks ran out with the sabbath year of AD 33, and AD 34 opened the next Jubilee, exactly ten cycles after the decree. That is the year commonly given for the stoning of Stephen, after which the believers “that were scattered abroad went every where preaching the word” (Acts 8:4), and the gospel went beyond Jerusalem. Every one of Gabriel’s six purposes, “to finish the transgression, and to make an end of sins, and to make reconciliation for iniquity, and to bring in everlasting righteousness, and to seal up the vision and prophecy, and to anoint the most Holy” (Daniel 9:24), is the Messiah’s own work, and He finished it right on time. The seventy weeks ran straight through. There is no gap in them, and the seventieth week is not waiting in the future.

An offer and an answer. When Jesus came, God made His people a real offer. “He came unto his own, and his own received him not. But as many as received him, to them gave he power to become the sons of God, even to them that believe on his name” (John 1:11-12). Jesus wept over Jerusalem because it did not recognize “the time of thy visitation” (Luke 19:44). God knew the answer before it came, but He did not force it; every person is free to choose (Deuteronomy 30:19). And God had His plan ready from the beginning: Jesus was “foreordained before the foundation of the world” as the Lamb who would redeem us (1 Peter 1:19-20).

Some of God’s promises came with a condition, an “if.” God explained it through Jeremiah: “At what instant I shall speak concerning a nation, and concerning a kingdom, to build and to plant it; If it do evil in my sight, that it obey not my voice, then I will repent of the good, wherewith I said I would benefit them” (Jeremiah 18:9-10). Some promises to Israel were set aside when the offer was refused, such as the temple Ezekiel was shown (Ezekiel 43:10-11), which was never built that way. But God’s purpose did not fail. It passed into a greater fulfillment in Christ, for “if ye be Christ’s, then are ye Abraham’s seed, and heirs according to the promise” (Galatians 3:29). Jew and Gentile together form one olive tree (Romans 11:17-24).

Daniel’s two messages. Daniel received two kinds of message. The first, in chapter 9, was open: “Know therefore and understand” (Daniel 9:25). It told of the Messiah’s first coming. The second was sealed for later: “But thou, O Daniel, shut up the words, and seal the book, even to the time of the end” (Daniel 12:4). It holds the end-time count of 1,290 and 1,335 days (Daniel 12:11-12). Because that count starts from an event and not from a calendar date, no one can use it to set a date ahead of time.

The abomination of desolation: two uses, never merged. An abomination is something God hates, and desolation means ruin. Daniel uses the phrase in two different prophecies, and each comes true once.

The first use has already happened. Matthew records Jesus saying the abomination would “stand in the holy place” (Matthew 24:15). Luke, writing for Gentile readers, tells us what that looked like: “And when ye shall see Jerusalem compassed with armies, then know that the desolation thereof is nigh. Then let them which are in Judaea flee to the mountains” (Luke 21:20-21). The believers who heeded Jesus fled, and in AD 70 Rome destroyed the city, as Daniel had foretold: “the people of the prince that shall come shall destroy the city and the sanctuary” (Daniel 9:26).

But AD 70 was not the end. In the same sermon Jesus said a long span would follow: “and Jerusalem shall be trodden down of the Gentiles, until the times of the Gentiles be fulfilled” (Luke 21:24). From Matthew 24:21 on, He turns to the great tribulation before His return, “such as was not since the beginning of the world to this time, no, nor ever shall be” (Matthew 24:21).

The second use is still ahead, and we will meet it again at day 1,290.

5. The Long Wait: Where We Are Now

After the seventy weeks, the gospel went out to the world, and the church entered a long wait.

The 1,260 years. For 1,260 years, the church lived in the wilderness, persecuted and kept alive by God (Revelation 12:6, 14). Daniel saw the power behind that persecution as a “little horn” that would “wear out the saints of the most High” (Daniel 7:25). In my reading of history, that little horn is the papacy as a system of power, which held both church and state in its hands from 538 until its dominion was taken away in 1798. This is about a system of power, not about the people in any church. God has His people in every church, and He calls them by name (Revelation 18:4).

The court sits. Daniel saw what would happen when those years ended: “But the judgment shall sit, and they shall take away his dominion, to consume and to destroy it unto the end” (Daniel 7:26). He saw the heavenly court gather: “the judgment was set, and the books were opened” (Daniel 7:10). And he saw the Son of man come before the Father, not to the earth, to receive the kingdom: “and, behold, one like the Son of man came with the clouds of heaven, and came to the Ancient of days, and they brought him near before him. And there was given him dominion, and glory, and a kingdom” (Daniel 7:13-14). John saw the same scene from the other side: the Lamb “came and took the book out of the right hand of him that sat upon the throne” (Revelation 5:7). As I read it, this happened after the 1,260 years ended; Scripture gives no year. In my study of Daniel 8, the 2,300 days end in the autumn of 1844 and open heaven’s Day of Atonement, the judgment that begins with God’s own house (1 Peter 4:17). That is never a date for Christ’s return.

Seals one to three are open. Once the Lamb took the book, He began opening its seals. As I read it, the first three seals have opened since 1798, and they are still running today. We will look at each one in the next sections.

We are in the last days, but not yet at the end. Scripture says the last days began at Pentecost (Acts 2:16-17; Hebrews 1:2). But we are not yet at what Daniel calls “the time of the end,” and the fourth seal, which starts the great tribulation, has not opened. If it had, a fourth of the earth would already be gone (Revelation 6:8). God is still holding back the winds of harm, for a reason we will see shortly.

6. The Pattern: Seals, Trumpets and Bowls

Here is the key that unlocks the rest of Revelation. Many readers try to line up the seven seals, the seven trumpets and the seven bowls one after another, as twenty-one events in a row. But Revelation does not tell the story that way. The seals are the larger campaigns. The trumpets and the bowls fall inside them.

Revelation gives us a strong clue that these are layers of one story, not three separate stories. Look at how each series ends:

  • The sixth seal: “and, lo, there was a great earthquake... And the heaven departed as a scroll when it is rolled together; and every mountain and island were moved out of their places” (Revelation 6:12, 14).
  • The seventh trumpet: “and there were lightnings, and voices, and thunderings, and an earthquake, and great hail” (Revelation 11:19).
  • The seventh bowl: “and there was a great earthquake, such as was not since men were upon the earth, so mighty an earthquake, and so great... And every island fled away, and the mountains were not found” (Revelation 16:18, 20).

The same earthquake, the same great hail, the same islands and mountains moved. The seventh bowl’s earthquake is the greatest that will ever happen, so it cannot be one of three different earthquakes. The sixth seal and the seventh trumpet even use the same words for the same moment: “For the great day of his wrath is come” (Revelation 6:17) and “thy wrath is come, and the time of the dead, that they should be judged” (Revelation 11:18). They are three views of one ending.

Here is the whole pattern in one place:

Notice the difference between the trumpets and the bowls. The trumpets strike “the third part” (Revelation 8:7-12). They are partial, because they are warnings, and God is still calling people to repent. The bowls hold nothing back. They are “the seven last plagues; for in them is filled up the wrath of God” (Revelation 15:1), poured out “without mixture” (Revelation 14:10). Between the two stands the door of mercy, which closes when the seventh trumpet begins to sound.

Revelation also repeats itself on purpose. It often gives the big picture and then comes back to fill in details, as Daniel tells of the same kingdoms in chapters 2, 7 and 8. Revelation 12, for example, steps all the way back to Christ’s birth. So when a later chapter seems to start over, it is usually showing the same events from another angle.

7. The Seals: Christ’s Campaign

Only one is worthy to open the book: “Weep not: behold, the Lion of the tribe of Juda, the Root of David, hath prevailed to open the book, and to loose the seven seals thereof” (Revelation 5:5). Every rider acts only by what is “given” to him (Revelation 6:2, 4, 8). Nothing happens unless the Lamb opens it.

The horses are not Satan’s. They are God’s Spirit going out into the world. John saw the Lamb “having seven horns and seven eyes, which are the seven Spirits of God sent forth into all the earth” (Revelation 5:6). Zechariah saw horses of the same colors, and the angel explained them: “These are the four spirits of the heavens, which go forth from standing before the Lord of all the earth” (Zechariah 6:5). God’s work is always done the same way: “Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit, saith the LORD of hosts” (Zechariah 4:6).

The first seal: the white horse. “And I saw, and behold a white horse: and he that sat on him had a bow; and a crown was given unto him: and he went forth conquering, and to conquer” (Revelation 6:2). This is the Lamb Himself, going out in victory through the gospel. The word “prevailed” in Revelation 5:5 and the word “conquering” here are the same Greek word (nikaō; Greek note, outside the KJV text). His crown is a victor’s crown, the same kind of crown Christ wears in Revelation 14:14. His bow is the Messiah’s bow: “Thy bow was made quite naked, according to the oaths of the tribes, even thy word” (Habakkuk 3:9). And His campaign carries out His own words: “And the gospel must first be published among all nations” (Mark 13:10).

The second seal: the red horse. “And there went out another horse that was red: and power was given to him that sat thereon to take peace from the earth, and that they should kill one another: and there was given unto him a great sword” (Revelation 6:4). The great sword is the Word of God, “the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God” (Ephesians 6:17). Wherever the Word goes, it divides people, as Jesus said: “Think not that I am come to send peace on earth: I came not to send peace, but a sword” (Matthew 10:34). Those who hate the truth persecute those who hold it. As I read history, this seal fits the age after 1798, when the Bible was printed and put into ordinary households around the world, and the persecution that followed.

The third seal: the black horse. “And I beheld, and lo a black horse; and he that sat on him had a pair of balances in his hand. And I heard a voice in the midst of the four beasts say, A measure of wheat for a penny, and three measures of barley for a penny; and see thou hurt not the oil and the wine” (Revelation 6:5-6). The balances are God’s scales of judgment: “Thou art weighed in the balances, and art found wanting” (Daniel 5:27). Heaven’s court is weighing every life. The penny was a day’s wage (Matthew 20:2), and God provides for His workers while judgment goes on. The oil and the wine, His Spirit and His covenant, are protected.

The pause before the harm. Before the fourth seal opens, God holds everything back: “And after these things I saw four angels standing on the four corners of the earth, holding the four winds of the earth, that the wind should not blow on the earth, nor on the sea, nor on any tree... Saying, Hurt not the earth, neither the sea, nor the trees, till we have sealed the servants of our God in their foreheads” (Revelation 7:1, 3). God seals His servants, the 144,000, drawn from every people, before any harm is released. As I read it, this is when the latter rain begins: the second great outpouring of the Holy Spirit, like the first at Pentecost, ripening the harvest before the Lord returns. James wrote: “Behold, the husbandman waiteth for the precious fruit of the earth, and hath long patience for it, until he receive the early and latter rain” (James 5:7). Every believer is already sealed by the Holy Spirit on believing: “ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise” (Ephesians 1:13). The 144,000 are “the servants of our God” before the angel seals them, so as I read it, this is a second sealing for their task: the Spirit given in full, so that they carry the last message faithfully, “And in their mouth was found no guile” (Revelation 14:5).

The fourth seal: the pale horse. “And I looked, and behold a pale horse: and his name that sat on him was Death, and Hell followed with him. And power was given unto them over the fourth part of the earth, to kill with sword, and with hunger, and with death, and with the beasts of the earth” (Revelation 6:8). This seal is still future. When it opens, the great tribulation begins, and the first four trumpets sound inside it.

The fifth seal: the souls under the altar. “I saw under the altar the souls of them that were slain for the word of God, and for the testimony which they held” (Revelation 6:9). They are the faithful martyrs of every age, and they are told to “rest yet for a little season, until their fellowservants also and their brethren, that should be killed as they were, should be fulfilled” (Revelation 6:11). The fifth and sixth trumpets sound inside this seal.

The sixth seal: the great day. This seal holds the seventh trumpet’s days, the seven bowls, and Christ’s appearing at the last trump. It ends with the great earthquake and the sky rolled back, and the world’s leaders hiding “from the face of him that sitteth on the throne, and from the wrath of the Lamb” (Revelation 6:16).

The seventh seal: silence. “And when he had opened the seventh seal, there was silence in heaven about the space of half an hour” (Revelation 8:1). Many readers take the seven trumpets to come out of this seal, because they are mentioned next. As I read it, the seventh seal is opened last of all, after the thousand years, for three reasons. First, the sixth seal already reaches the end: every mountain and island is moved, and “the great day of his wrath is come” (Revelation 6:14, 17). Trumpets that came after it would still be burning “the third part of trees” (Revelation 8:7) after the world had already ended. Second, Revelation 8:1 closes the list of seals in a single line, and verse 2 opens a new scene with “And I saw,” the way John begins new visions all through the book; the chapter and verse numbers were added more than a thousand years after John wrote. Third, Revelation often steps back in time, as chapter 12 goes back to Christ’s birth, so the order John saw the visions in is not always the order of the events. Why after the thousand years? A sealed book can be read only when its last seal is broken, and the book Scripture shows opened at the very end is the book of life (Revelation 20:12). Scripture does not say in so many words that the sealed book is the book of life. That link is my reading, and I give the reasons in The Books Were Opened.

8. The Main Figures in the Story

Revelation tells the end of the story through a small cast of figures. Scripture names some of them outright. The rest are read from how Scripture uses the same words elsewhere, and I say so when it is my reading.

The Lamb is Jesus, “the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world” (John 1:29). He opens every seal.

The dragon is Satan: “that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan, which deceiveth the whole world” (Revelation 12:9). In this research he is THE Antichrist, the great enemy behind every other enemy of God’s people. As I read it, he is held now in the bottomless pit, a spirit realm. Peter says God “spared not the angels that sinned, but cast them down to hell, and delivered them into chains of darkness, to be reserved unto judgment” (2 Peter 2:4), and the devils begged Jesus “that he would not command them to go out into the deep” (Luke 8:31). The angel told John that the beast “was, and is not; and shall ascend out of the bottomless pit” (Revelation 17:8), and the king over the locusts is “the angel of the bottomless pit” (Revelation 9:11). From there Satan works in the world only by influence, “the spirit that now worketh in the children of disobedience” (Ephesians 2:2). At the fifth trumpet he is given the key and comes up out of the pit (Revelation 9:1-2). Revelation 20 then describes a later binding, for the thousand years.

Babylon, the beast from the sea, is a one-world church-state that rises during the great tribulation (Revelation 13:1-10). John sees it as a woman, a false religious rule, riding a scarlet beast, the government of ten kings: “the woman which thou sawest is that great city, which reigneth over the kings of the earth” (Revelation 17:18). “The dragon gave him his power, and his seat, and great authority” (Revelation 13:2).

Its seven heads are explained by the angel: “The seven heads are seven mountains, on which the woman sitteth” (Revelation 17:9). Scripture uses mountains for systems of worship. A woman told Jesus, “Our fathers worshipped in this mountain” (John 4:20). Paul made a mountain stand for a whole covenant and called it a mother: “the one from the mount Sinai, which gendereth to bondage” (Galatians 4:24). And the prophets pictured false worship as a harlot on the mountains: “she is gone up upon every high mountain and under every green tree, and there hath played the harlot” (Jeremiah 3:6). So, as I read it, the seven heads are the false religious systems of history, and the woman sitting on them is false religion enthroned. Scripture does not name the seven, and neither does this paper.

The seven heads are also seven kings. The angel adds: “And there are seven kings: five are fallen, and one is, and the other is not yet come; and when he cometh, he must continue a short space” (Revelation 17:10). In Daniel a king stands for his whole kingdom: “These great beasts, which are four, are four kings” (Daniel 7:17), and “The fourth beast shall be the fourth kingdom” (Daniel 7:23). So, as I read it, the seven kings are the same seven systems as the seven mountains, seen as powers that rule over people. Five had already fallen by John’s day. Scripture does not name them, and neither do I. Nor does it name the seventh, which was still to come.

The one that “is.” In John’s day the believers were known simply as “the way” (Acts 9:2; 19:23; 24:14), and they were first called Christians at Antioch (Acts 11:26). The church of the apostles was not a beast. But Scripture warned that a falling away would grow from inside it. Paul told the elders at Ephesus: “For I know this, that after my departing shall grievous wolves enter in among you, not sparing the flock. Also of your own selves shall men arise, speaking perverse things, to draw away disciples after them” (Acts 20:29-30). He said the seed was already there: “For the mystery of iniquity doth already work” (2 Thessalonians 2:7). As I read it, the sixth head is the visible church as an earthly institution, which existed in John’s day and which, step by step, joined itself to Caesar and became something its first members would not have recognized.

History shows how that happened. These are the outlines of what the records show, not Scripture:

  • In the New Testament each local church was led by elders, also called bishops or overseers (Acts 20:17, 28; Titus 1:5-7).
  • Around AD 110, Ignatius of Antioch urged every church to follow a single bishop, and he is the first writer known to use the word “catholic,” meaning universal, of the church.
  • The church was persecuted by Rome until 313, when the Edict of Milan granted Christians toleration.
  • In 325, Emperor Constantine called the Council of Nicaea. An emperor was now gathering the church’s leaders.
  • In 380, Emperor Theodosius made the Nicene faith the official religion of the empire. Church and state were joined.
  • In the 400s, Leo I, bishop of Rome (440-461), claimed authority over the whole church as Peter’s successor.
  • In 533-534, Emperor Justinian’s law code named the bishop of Rome head of all the churches, with the emperor’s power behind his rulings. By 538 that joint power could be enforced in Rome itself, the start of the 1,260 years (see Section 5).

Jesus had kept the two apart: “Render therefore unto Caesar the things which are Caesar’s; and unto God the things that are God’s” (Matthew 22:21). The system that joined them is what Daniel saw as the “little horn” ruling over kings, and what Revelation shows as a head, a religion. The two symbols show two roles of one power. Unto Caesar, and Unto God tells this history in full, with its sources.

The wounded head. “And I saw one of his heads as it were wounded to death; and his deadly wound was healed: and all the world wondered after the beast” (Revelation 13:3). As I read it, this is that sixth head. The history behind it is strong: in 1798 the papacy’s ruler was taken prisoner and died in exile, and the wound looked fatal; from 1929, when Vatican City was recognized as a sovereign state, it began to recover. Its full healing, and the world’s wonder after it, are still future, when Babylon rises. Notice too that this is one head among seven, on a beast that also carries ten crowned kings. Babylon is the false religion and false government of the whole world joined together, so this prophecy does not single out any one church. I say this with care. It is about a system of power, not about Catholic people, and God has His people in every church and calls them out by name: “Come out of her, my people, that ye be not partakers of her sins, and that ye receive not of her plagues” (Revelation 18:4).

The ten kings are the ten horns: “And the ten horns which thou sawest are ten kings, which have received no kingdom as yet; but receive power as kings one hour with the beast” (Revelation 17:12). As I read it, they form a one-world government after the first trumpets’ destruction, trying to restore order, and the woman rides their government for a time. Later they turn on her: “these shall hate the whore, and shall make her desolate and naked, and shall eat her flesh, and burn her with fire” (Revelation 17:16). Daniel saw them as the toes of iron and clay that “shall not cleave one to another” (Daniel 2:43).

The false prophet, the beast from the earth, “had two horns like a lamb, and he spake as a dragon” (Revelation 13:11). He looks gentle but speaks for Satan. He is a being whom Satan possesses and works through, as “then entered Satan into Judas” (Luke 22:3). He does great wonders, sets up an image, and enforces the mark (Revelation 13:13-17). Scripture does not say who he is, and I do not guess.

The eighth. Behind them all stands Satan himself. The angel says of the sea beast in its final form: “the beast that was, and is not, even he is the eighth” (Revelation 17:11), the one who “shall ascend out of the bottomless pit” (Revelation 17:8). In the end Satan reigns openly through Babylon, the hand inside the glove.

9. The Great Tribulation, Step by Step

Daniel gives the count for the end: “And from the time that the daily sacrifice shall be taken away, and the abomination that maketh desolate set up, there shall be a thousand two hundred and ninety days. Blessed is he that waiteth, and cometh to the thousand three hundred and five and thirty days” (Daniel 12:11-12). Revelation adds the 1,260 days of the two witnesses (Revelation 11:3). As I read it, the 1,260, the 1,290 and the 1,335 are one count, from one starting day. Here is how the story unfolds, in the order this study finds in Revelation.

Day 1: the censer and the fourth seal. “And the angel took the censer, and filled it with fire of the altar, and cast it into the earth: and there were voices, and thunderings, and lightnings, and an earthquake” (Revelation 8:5). The fourth seal opens, the winds are released, and the great tribulation begins.

The first four trumpets. Hail and fire, a burning mountain cast into the sea, a burning star falling on the rivers, and darkness strike a third of the earth, the sea, the waters and the sky (Revelation 8:7-12). They strike the very things the gospel calls the world to honor God for: “worship him that made heaven, and earth, and the sea, and the fountains of waters” (Revelation 14:7). Believers may suffer, and some may die, in these calamities, as people always have in disasters (Luke 13:4-5). But that is not God’s wrath on them. Scripture never calls the trumpets “wrath.”

A picture of the first trumpets (speculative). In July 1994, the world watched something that had never been seen before. Comet Shoemaker-Levy 9, already torn by Jupiter’s gravity into about twenty-one pieces, struck the planet one piece after another from July 16 to 22. The impacts left dark scars, the largest about the size of the earth, visible for months even in small backyard telescopes. It was the first collision between two bodies in the solar system that people had ever watched.

I want to be careful here. Scripture does not mention this comet, and it does not say it was a sign. Calling it a sign is speculation, and nothing in this paper depends on it. But it does give a vivid picture of what the first trumpets of Revelation could look like when they strike the earth: “there followed hail and fire mingled with blood, and they were cast upon the earth” (Revelation 8:7); “as it were a great mountain burning with fire was cast into the sea” (Revelation 8:8); “there fell a great star from heaven, burning as it were a lamp” (Revelation 8:10); “and the third part of the sun was smitten, and the third part of the moon, and the third part of the stars” (Revelation 8:12). Jesus said there would be “signs in the sun, and in the moon, and in the stars” before His coming (Luke 21:25).

Babylon rises. As I read it, the ten kings form a one-world government in response to the destruction, and the sea beast rises with the woman riding it. Its “forty and two months” (Revelation 13:5) run alongside the witnesses’ 1,260 days. The false prophet rises with it, honoring it and working wonders (Revelation 13:11-14).

The two witnesses. “And I will give power unto my two witnesses, and they shall prophesy a thousand two hundred and threescore days, clothed in sackcloth” (Revelation 11:3). As I read it, the two witnesses are the Holy Spirit and the Word of God, speaking through God’s sealed servants. Scripture pairs them as witnesses: “My spirit that is upon thee, and my words which I have put in thy mouth, shall not depart out of thy mouth” (Isaiah 59:21). They carry the everlasting gospel “unto every nation, and kindred, and tongue, and people” (Revelation 14:6), and many come to Christ through them.

The fifth seal and the fifth trumpet. “And I saw a star fall from heaven unto the earth: and to him was given the key of the bottomless pit. And he opened the bottomless pit” (Revelation 9:1-2). As I read it, the fallen star is Satan, whom Jesus saw “as lightning fall from heaven” (Luke 10:18), and he comes up out of the pit with his host, for their king is “the angel of the bottomless pit” (Revelation 9:11). For five months they torment “only those men which have not the seal of God in their foreheads” (Revelation 9:4-5), and they may not kill. God’s people are spared, as Israel was in Egypt, where “all the children of Israel had light in their dwellings” (Exodus 10:23).

The sixth trumpet. Four fallen angels bound at the Euphrates are loosed, “which were prepared for an hour, and a day, and a month, and a year, for to slay the third part of men” (Revelation 9:15). God set that moment in advance, which shows He, not Satan, is in control. And still, “the rest of the men which were not killed by these plagues yet repented not” (Revelation 9:20).

The man of sin and the mark. As I read it, Satan, working through the false prophet, exalts himself as God, sitting “in the temple of God, shewing himself that he is God” (2 Thessalonians 2:4), “in the glorious holy mountain” (Daniel 11:45). The false prophet sets up an image and enforces the mark, “that no man might buy or sell, save he that had the mark, or the name of the beast, or the number of his name” (Revelation 13:17). The mark and the seal are about whom you worship and whom you trust for your daily bread, not a day of the week. No one takes the mark by accident. The warning goes out first, “with a loud voice, If any man worship the beast and his image, and receive his mark in his forehead, or in his hand, The same shall drink of the wine of the wrath of God” (Revelation 14:9-10).

Day 1,260: the witnesses finish. “And when they shall have finished their testimony, the beast that ascendeth out of the bottomless pit shall make war against them, and shall overcome them, and kill them” (Revelation 11:7). After three and a half days, “the Spirit of life from God entered into them, and they stood upon their feet” (Revelation 11:11), and they are taken up in the sight of their enemies. As I read it, this is a public sign, and the slain messengers are presented to God as “firstfruits” before the harvest (Revelation 14:4). It is not the gathering of the church.

The seventh trumpet: the door closes. “The second woe is past; and, behold, the third woe cometh quickly. And the seventh angel sounded; and there were great voices in heaven, saying, The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of his Christ; and he shall reign for ever and ever” (Revelation 11:14-15). The seventh trumpet is not one short blast. It sounds through a span of days: “But in the days of the voice of the seventh angel, when he shall begin to sound, the mystery of God should be finished” (Revelation 10:7). When it begins, mercy’s door closes, as it did for Noah when “the LORD shut him in” (Genesis 7:16). “He that is unjust, let him be unjust still: and he which is filthy, let him be filthy still: and he that is righteous, let him be righteous still: and he that is holy, let him be holy still” (Revelation 22:11). And “no man was able to enter into the temple, till the seven plagues of the seven angels were fulfilled” (Revelation 15:8). Daniel saw the same moment: “And at that time shall Michael stand up, the great prince which standeth for the children of thy people” (Daniel 12:1).

The first five bowls. Now, and only now, God’s wrath is poured out. The bowls fall “upon the men which had the mark of the beast, and upon them which worshipped his image” (Revelation 16:2): sores, the sea turned to blood, the rivers turned to blood, scorching heat, and darkness on the throne of the sea beast (Revelation 16:2-11). God’s people are still on the earth, but He keeps them, as He kept Israel in Goshen: “And I will sever in that day the land of Goshen, in which my people dwell, that no swarms of flies shall be there” (Exodus 8:22).

Day 1,290: the death decree. This is the second abomination of desolation. The false prophet causes “that as many as would not worship the image of the beast should be killed” (Revelation 13:15). As in Esther’s day, when a decree set one day to destroy all the Jews (Esther 3:13), God’s people face a set day of death. Most translations read the daily taken away and the abomination set up as one event at the start of the 1,290 days. As I read Daniel 12:11, they are the two ends of the count. Young’s Literal Translation renders it: “from the time of the turning aside of the perpetual [sacrifice], and to the giving out of the desolating abomination” (Hebrew note, outside the KJV text). “From” the one, “and to” the other: the daily is taken away at the start, and the decree is set up on day 1,290. “The daily,” as I read it, is the open worship of God’s people, suppressed while the holy city is trodden down (Daniel 8:13; Revelation 11:2). This is this research’s reading, and I give it as such. God’s people may flee and hide, as they always have: “Come, my people, enter thou into thy chambers, and shut thy doors about thee: hide thyself as it were for a little moment, until the indignation be overpast” (Isaiah 26:20). And God’s promise stands: “at that time thy people shall be delivered, every one that shall be found written in the book” (Daniel 12:1).

The sixth bowl. The Euphrates is dried up, “that the way of the kings of the east might be prepared” (Revelation 16:12). History records that ancient Babylon fell when its river was turned aside, and the end-time Babylon falls the same way. As I read it, the kings of the east are the Father and the Son coming with the armies of heaven. Three unclean spirits gather the kings of the earth “to the battle of that great day of God Almighty” (Revelation 16:14), and in the middle of it all, Jesus speaks to His people: “Behold, I come as a thief. Blessed is he that watcheth, and keepeth his garments” (Revelation 16:15). His people are still here, still watching.

Babylon falls. The ten kings see at last who has been behind the false prophet, and they turn on the woman and burn her (Revelation 17:16-17). Babylon falls in “one hour” (Revelation 18:10).

The seventh bowl: “It is done.” “And the seventh angel poured out his vial into the air; and there came a great voice out of the temple of heaven, from the throne, saying, It is done” (Revelation 16:17). The greatest earthquake ever, the great hail, and the islands and mountains gone (Revelation 16:18-21).

Day 1,335: the last trump. The seventh trumpet’s days end with its last note, and Christ appears. “In a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trump: for the trumpet shall sound, and the dead shall be raised incorruptible, and we shall be changed” (1 Corinthians 15:52). Revelation pictures that day as two harvests: first the Son of man reaps the earth and gathers His people, then the vine of the earth is cast “into the great winepress of the wrath of God” (Revelation 14:14-20).

Here is the same story at a glance. Scripture gives the 1,260, the 1,290 and the 1,335 days. Reading them as one count from one starting day, and placing each event within its seal, is this study’s reading.

10. Doesn’t Counting the Days Go Against Jesus’ Words?

Jesus said, “But of that day and hour knoweth no man, no, not the angels of heaven, but my Father only” (Matthew 24:36). Does counting Daniel’s days break that rule? Jesus answered in the very next verses: “But as the days of Noe were, so shall also the coming of the Son of man be. For as in the days that were before the flood they were eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage, until the day that Noe entered into the ark, And knew not until the flood came, and took them all away” (Matthew 24:37-39).

The world did not know. But Noah did, once God told him: “For yet seven days, and I will cause it to rain upon the earth forty days and forty nights” (Genesis 7:4). No one could know the day before the count began. Once it began, Noah could count to it, while the world was still caught by surprise. Paul says the same: “But ye, brethren, are not in darkness, that that day should overtake you as a thief” (1 Thessalonians 5:4).

That is why this research publishes no future dates. Daniel’s count starts from an event, the opening of the fourth seal, and that event has not happened. Until it does, our call is to be ready, not to guess.

11. Christ Appears and Gathers His People

At the last trump, Christ comes in the clouds, and every eye sees Him (Matthew 24:30; Revelation 1:7). “For the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout, with the voice of the archangel, and with the trump of God: and the dead in Christ shall rise first: Then we which are alive and remain shall be caught up together with them in the clouds, to meet the Lord in the air: and so shall we ever be with the Lord” (1 Thessalonians 4:16-17).

This is the one gathering of God’s people, after the tribulation, at the last trump. That timing does not mean the church suffers God’s wrath. God’s wrath is the seven last plagues, poured on those with the mark, while His people are kept through them. “For God hath not appointed us to wrath, but to obtain salvation by our Lord Jesus Christ” (1 Thessalonians 5:9).

He gathers His people to Himself and takes them where He is, as He promised: “I will come again, and receive you unto myself; that where I am, there ye may be also” (John 14:3). The wicked who are still alive are slain at His coming (Revelation 19:21), the sea beast, Babylon’s government, and the false prophet “were cast alive into a lake of fire burning with brimstone” (Revelation 19:20), and Satan is bound (Revelation 20:1-3).

Daniel adds one more detail: “And many of them that sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake, some to everlasting life, and some to shame and everlasting contempt” (Daniel 12:2). As I read it, those awakened to shame, including “they also which pierced him” (Revelation 1:7), are raised mortal, like Lazarus, not to resurrection life; they see Him come, as He told the council (Matthew 26:64), and they die with the rest of the wicked at His coming.

12. A Thousand Years of Rest, and a New Beginning

The thousand years. Now the earth lies empty and quiet for a thousand years, keeping its long sabbath, like the land that rested while Israel was in exile (Jeremiah 4:23-27; Leviticus 26:34-35). With no one left alive to deceive, Satan is bound. The saints reign with Christ in heaven and take part in the judgment: “Do ye not know that the saints shall judge the world?” (1 Corinthians 6:2). As I read it, Jesus uses those years to show His people why each of His judgments was right, so that every question is answered.

After the thousand years. Christ comes with all His saints, and the holy city, New Jerusalem, comes down from God (Zechariah 14:4-5; Revelation 21:2). “But the rest of the dead lived not again until the thousand years were finished” (Revelation 20:5); now they are raised. Satan is loosed and gathers them, “Gog and Magog... the number of whom is as the sand of the sea,” and they surround the city (Revelation 20:8-9), but they cannot enter it, for nothing that defiles may enter (Revelation 21:27). The books of every life are opened (Revelation 20:12).

The seventh seal. Then the last seal is broken, and the book of life is opened: “And when he had opened the seventh seal, there was silence in heaven about the space of half an hour” (Revelation 8:1). As I read it, the names the Father wrote in His book match the Son’s judgments, name for name, so that every mouth is stopped and God is shown to be just.

The second death and the new earth. “And fire came down from God out of heaven, and devoured them” (Revelation 20:9). The devil, and everyone not found written in the book of life, are cast into the lake of fire, which destroys both soul and body; “This is the second death” (Revelation 20:14), and no one is raised from it. Then God makes all things new: “And I saw a new heaven and a new earth: for the first heaven and the first earth were passed away” (Revelation 21:1). The promise from the beginning is kept: God dwells with His people, and He is their God (Revelation 21:3).

Look back at the pattern of God’s clock. Six days of work, then a sabbath, then a fresh start. Six periods of history, then the thousand years as the earth’s sabbath, then the new creation as the great Jubilee, when everything goes back to its rightful Owner and all who are His go free (Leviticus 25:10; Romans 8:21). The CJC builds this into its very math: the fiftieth year is also the first year of the next cycle. One order ends, and another begins: “Behold, I make all things new” (Revelation 21:5).

13. How to Prepare, by God’s Word

God did not give us this story to frighten us. He gave it so that anyone who is willing can be ready. Here is how Scripture tells us to prepare.

  • Come to Jesus now. The seal of God is not a ritual or a day of the week. It is His Spirit, given to everyone who believes: “In whom ye also trusted, after that ye heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation: in whom also after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise” (Ephesians 1:13).
  • Know the Word for yourself. “Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path” (Psalm 119:105). Read the verses in this paper and check them, as the Bereans did, who “searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so” (Acts 17:11).
  • Trust God, not the system, for your daily bread. The mark will be offered as the way to buy and sell. God promises His own: “bread shall be given him; his waters shall be sure” (Isaiah 33:16), and “seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you” (Matthew 6:33).
  • Refuse the mark. No one will take it by accident. The warning comes first, with a loud voice (Revelation 14:9).
  • Come out of Babylon. “Come out of her, my people, that ye be not partakers of her sins, and that ye receive not of her plagues” (Revelation 18:4).
  • Watch and pray, and do not fear. “And when these things begin to come to pass, then look up, and lift up your heads; for your redemption draweth nigh” (Luke 21:28).

God warns before He judges. He waits before He shuts the door. And He keeps His own through the storm. He did it for Noah, for Israel in Egypt, and for the believers who fled Jerusalem. He will do it again. “The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance” (2 Peter 3:9).
